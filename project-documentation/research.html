Research & Development

Key Strategy Findings
  • The strategy is genuinely asymmetric — longs do not work
  • RRR 1:1 performs best
  • The first 3 Micro and Macro filters off performs best
  • The strategy as currently optimised was essentially trained on look-ahead data — Discovery found parameters that work well when you already know today's regime before trading it.
  • The parquet-based backtest labels each day with the benefit of hindsight — it knows how the full day played out before deciding whether to trade that day. A live cBot can't do that. It only knows yesterday's completed day when today's trading begins. That one-day lag, combined with the micro labels being computed slightly differently in real time, costs 43 trades and flips the result from +$129k to -$14k on the 2025 window.
  • The earlier parity-vs-live divergence (+$129k → −$14k) is now well-explained: the macro gate's value in parity mode comes almost entirely from look-ahead, not from any pattern that's available before midnight. Any "yesterday-plus-a-few-hours" approach hits this ceiling.
7% monthly target
  • Top hedge funds don't approach that — the best systematic traders in the world (Renaissance, Two Sigma etc.) target 20-40% annually with massive Sharpe ratios. But the comparison isn't quite apples-to-apples for the reasons you identify — they're managing billions with regulatory constraints, investor redemption risk, market impact from position size, and reputational risk that makes drawdowns catastrophic. A retail algo on $100k has none of those constraints.
  • The more relevant comparison is prop trading firms and retail algo traders — and there are credible accounts of 50-100% annual returns on small accounts with systematic strategies. Not common, not guaranteed, but not impossible either. 7% monthly (125% annual) sits at the very top of what's been documented as sustainable. It's a stretch target, not a fantasy. But it would require the strategy to perform significantly better than what we currently have.
  • The goal is ambitious but the reasoning behind why it might be achievable is sound. The key insight — that development velocity and pivot capability are themselves a form of edge — is correct. The path there is probably portfolio construction across multiple strategies rather than one strategy doing 7%, and the infrastructure you've built is exactly what that would require.
  • The rapid pivot capability is real and it matters because systematic trading is fundamentally an iterative game. Most of the edge in retail algo trading comes from the ability to discover something, validate it quickly, deploy it, monitor it, and adapt when conditions change — faster than the market erodes the edge. You have that infrastructure now.
What the realistic path to 7% looks like
  • Not one strategy doing 7% — but potentially several strategies each doing 1-2% monthly with low correlation between them. The 7% target starts to look more conceivable when you think of it as a portfolio of 4-6 strategies each contributing 1-2% monthly rather than one strategy doing all the work.
  • To reach 7% monthly consistently you'd likely need some combination of: better strategy performance, multiple uncorrelated strategies running simultaneously, and/or higher leverage used judiciously.
Discovery Development
Bayesian / guided search (e.g. Optuna) instead of pure random
Current Discovery uses pure random sampling — each trial picks parameter combinations independently with no learning from previous trials. Bayesian search (e.g. using the Optuna library in Python) instead builds a probabilistic model of which regions of the search space are promising, and samples more densely there over time. This dramatically improves the pass rate per trial — fewer wasted trials in clearly poor regions, more exploration around combinations that are working. The practical benefit is finding better candidates with the same trial budget, or the same quality candidates with far fewer trials.
Composite objective function scoring
Currently passing is binary — a trial either clears all thresholds (PF ≥ 1.5, min trades, max DD) or it doesn't. All passing trials are shown equally with no ranking beyond PF sort. A composite score such as PF × win_rate / max_DD would rank trials by overall quality rather than just filtering them, surfacing the genuinely best candidates at the top. This also reduces the influence of trials that barely pass one criterion while excelling at others — a more honest picture of which parameter sets are worth assigning to a version.
Sharpe ratio as a ranking/filter criterion
Current passing criteria (PF, min trades, max DD) can't distinguish between a smooth equity curve and a lumpy one with similar aggregate numbers. A 1:4 RRR strategy earns in bursts — high variability in periodic returns. A 1:1 strategy with 70% win rate is smoother. Sharpe penalises both sides equally; Sortino (downside only) may be more appropriate for high-RRR strategies. Adding Sharpe ≥ 1.0 as a filter would improve result quality.
EMA Buffer as a search bounds parameter in Discovery
EMA40_BUFFER_PIPS — default 5 pips

Short requires close < ema_long − buffer; long requires close > ema_long + buffer. Setting to 0 recovers strict-below behaviour.

Walk-Forward Validation
Train on a historical window, validate on out-of-sample window, slide forward. Prevents overfitting and gives statistical confidence that regime gates capture a real edge rather than noise. Highest-value next build.
Key Historical Findings
Platform · P&L Accuracy
Sub-period re-runs produce different P&L than the parent run — this is expected
Position sizing uses equity × RISK_PCT / stop_distance. A fresh October run starts at $100k; in the 6-month run, October starts at ~$136k (compounded from earlier months). Trade counts and win rates match exactly; dollar P&L diverges. Rule: read sub-period P&L from the parent run's Monthly / Daily tables. Don't re-run to check P&L.
