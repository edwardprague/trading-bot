Initial Thoughts and Ideas

Pullbacks

The micro trends are the clear part, very easy to identify by design. The question is about the pullbacks, which is the unclear part, by design. The unpredictability of the pullback depth and timing is what causes retail traders to get stopped out and provide the liquidity.

  • When and how will the PBPP sweeps occur?
  • How far will the pull backs go? How many PBPP will be swept before continuing in the overall trend.
  • Will there be LTF PBPP sweeps or is the market interested in continuing efficiently to a HTF level?

Entry Thoughts

  • Expand current entry criteria (first fractal lower that the previous one) to include a head and shoulders pattern detection (first fractal lower that the previous one + lower fractal before the previous one).
  • Head and shoulders pattern - Shoulder + Head + Shoulder = Fractal 1 + Fractal 2 + Fractal 3 (enter trade)
  • For tighter entries it might be better to enter on the 4th fractal candle (if it's engulfing the previous candle) instead of the 5th, but that is an unconfirmed fractal and may not work as well.
  • This entry might work better in consolidation cycles than trending cycles.
  • The structure of the EMAs, height measurements, and pullback % of the recent fractals, could inform whether the price is consolidating or trending.

Potential Strategy Analysis Development

Bayesian / guided search (e.g. Optuna) instead of pure random
Current Discovery uses pure random sampling — each trial picks parameter combinations independently with no learning from previous trials. Bayesian search (e.g. using the Optuna library in Python) instead builds a probabilistic model of which regions of the search space are promising, and samples more densely there over time. This dramatically improves the pass rate per trial — fewer wasted trials in clearly poor regions, more exploration around combinations that are working. The practical benefit is finding better candidates with the same trial budget, or the same quality candidates with far fewer trials.
Composite objective function scoring
Currently passing is binary — a trial either clears all thresholds (PF ≥ 1.5, min trades, max DD) or it doesn't. All passing trials are shown equally with no ranking beyond PF sort. A composite score such as PF × win_rate / max_DD would rank trials by overall quality rather than just filtering them, surfacing the genuinely best candidates at the top. This also reduces the influence of trials that barely pass one criterion while excelling at others — a more honest picture of which parameter sets are worth assigning to a version.
Sharpe ratio as a ranking/filter criterion
Current passing criteria (PF, min trades, max DD) can't distinguish between a smooth equity curve and a lumpy one with similar aggregate numbers. A 1:4 RRR strategy earns in bursts — high variability in periodic returns. A 1:1 strategy with 70% win rate is smoother. Sharpe penalises both sides equally; Sortino (downside only) may be more appropriate for high-RRR strategies. Adding Sharpe ≥ 1.0 as a filter would improve result quality.
EMA Buffer as a search bounds parameter in Discovery
EMA40_BUFFER_PIPS — default 5 pips

Short requires close < ema_long − buffer; long requires close > ema_long + buffer. Setting to 0 recovers strict-below behaviour.

Walk-Forward Validation
Train on a historical window, validate on out-of-sample window, slide forward. Prevents overfitting and gives statistical confidence that regime gates capture a real edge rather than noise. Highest-value next build.
